## Large Language Models, Poetics, and Choreographic Thinking

## Introduction

Modern large language models (LLMs) generate text by traversing a high-dimensional latent space of language in a sequential, stepwise fashion. Each new word or line produced by an LLM can be seen as a step along a vector in this latent space. This process bears a striking conceptual resemblance to human creativity and improvisation.

Poet **William Carlos Williams** famously asserted that *“unless there is a new mind there cannot be a new line”*, highlighting how a shift in mindset is required to produce truly original language. Similarly, choreographer **William Forsythe’s** experimental dance techniques treat the body as a thinking tool that can generate novel movement through algorithmic improvisation. In both poetic creation and choreographic improvisation, as in LLM-driven text generation, we find a common theme: a linear sequence of actions or words emerging from a space of possibilities, guided by both constraints and creativity.

This essay explores the connections between the vectorial organization of language in LLMs, the improvisational nature of human thought (the *“mind is flat”* thesis), and the choreographic thinking exemplified by Forsythe’s work. Through this interdisciplinary lens, we examine how sequential vectorial linearity in a space of potential outcomes underpins reasoning, creativity, and the emergence of new “lines” of thought.

## Large Language Models: Sequential Trajectories in Latent Space

Large language models like GPT-3 or LLaMA operate in a mathematical latent space, where each token (word or subword) is represented as a high-dimensional vector. Rather than manipulating words directly, the model converts each token into an embedding—essentially a list of numbers—and processes these through numerous neural network layers. The final result is a hidden state vector that encapsulates the context, from which the model predicts the most likely next token. This prediction is then added to the sequence and the process repeats.

In effect, the LLM “thinks” by moving from one point to the next in this vector space, generating a sequence of vectors that correspond to a sequence of words. The model’s reasoning process thus takes the form of a path or trajectory through latent space, one token at a time, constrained by learned statistical relationships in language.

Each step in this trajectory is informed by the model’s training on vast amounts of text. Words or tokens that appear in similar contexts end up with similar vector representations, meaning the latent space encodes a rich web of relationships. For example, an LLM’s embedding for *“dog”* will lie near *“cat”* but far from *“chair”*, mirroring the fact that dogs and cats appear in analogous linguistic contexts. This geometry allows the model to make educated guesses about what comes next in a sentence.

The latent vectors function somewhat like coordinates on a high-dimensional map: clusters of related meanings form regions (e.g., animals, furniture), and directions in space capture relational semantics (famously, *king – man + woman ≈ queen* in vector arithmetic). In this way, the LLM’s internal vector space is a space of possibilities for language, where traversing one direction versus another yields different continuations of a text.

Critically, the generation process in LLMs is sequential and improvisational. The model has no overarching plan for an entire paragraph; it produces each new token based on what has come before, much like a jazz musician improvises the next bar of music by listening to the previous bars. Researchers have enhanced LLM performance by getting them to generate intermediate tokens that resemble a chain of reasoning steps—the so-called *chain-of-thought* prompts. In such cases, the LLM is effectively narrating its own stepwise process, mimicking the way a human reasoner might break down a problem.

The key point is that everything the model does is a result of moving through the latent vector space incrementally; each new “line” of output is the consequence of the model’s internal state (a mathematical vector) evolving in response to input and its own prior outputs.

## “A New Line Is a New Mind”: Poetics, Creativity, and the Finite Mind

Williams’s adage, *“unless there is a new mind there cannot be a new line”*, encapsulates a central idea in creativity: genuine innovation in language requires a transformation in perspective or consciousness. In the context of poetry, a *new line* represents not merely a line break or a fresh sentence, but a novel expression—a departure from cliché and from *“the old…repeating itself with recurring deadliness.”*

The *new mind* implies an inner change—a shift in perception, values, or attention—that makes original expression possible. Literary analysis of *Paterson* emphasizes that Williams was advocating for an inward renewal as the precursor to outward innovation in poetic form. Creativity, then, is not just a technical trick or formal novelty; it is rooted in conceptual transformation.

The finite human mind, with all its limitations and habitual patterns, must somehow be rebooted or reoriented to break out of repetition and produce a truly new line of poetry.

This poetic principle resonates with how language models and human improvisers work. An LLM, if naively applied, will tend to produce the statistical average of its training data, resulting in formulaic or stale outputs. To elicit creativity, one must introduce a form of a *new mind* into the model’s process—by adjusting temperature, altering prompts, or reframing tasks in unusual ways.

These interventions push the model’s next-token choices out of the most predictable rut, effectively shifting its trajectory in latent space to explore less-traveled regions—a rough analog of adopting a new mindset. In creative writing with AI, novel lines of text often emerge precisely when the model is guided to step outside the highest-probability continuations.

For human creators, the finite mind is constrained by prior knowledge, biases, and cognitive habits, yet can experience moments of insight that enable breakthroughs. Cognitive science describes creativity as a process of making new connections or reframing problems—essentially *re-vectoring* one’s mental space to find a path that was not obvious before. In this sense, both a poet and an AI language model face a comparable challenge: avoid mere regurgitation of the familiar.

## The Improvising Brain: Reasoning as On-the-Fly Construction

Is human thinking fundamentally improvisational? Cognitive scientist **Nick Chater** argues yes. In *The Mind Is Flat*, he suggests that our sense of having deep, hidden mental depths is illusory and that we essentially make up our minds as we go along. According to Chater, the brain *“just makes everything up as it goes along”*, with no vast reservoir of pre-formed beliefs underneath.

Our beliefs and perceptions are constructed on the fly from available cues. Reasoning and introspection resemble acts of storytelling—a kind of narrative improvisation. There is, in Chater’s view, *“nothing going on underneath”* our conscious reasoning; explanations and decisions are confabulated in real time.

This view dovetails with how LLMs generate responses. An LLM has no hidden self or agenda beyond the patterns encoded in its weights. When asked to produce text, it improvises based on statistical associations, much as Chater suggests humans do. Both rely on accumulated past experience—training data for the model, memory for the human—but neither guarantees a deep, stable doctrine underlying each response.

Human metacognition—thinking about our own thinking—may give us some steering ability, but even this may be another improvised narrative. When an LLM is prompted to *explain its reasoning*, it produces a plausible explanation because it has learned how explanations are worded, not because it accessed an internal reasoning trace. This simulated metacognition underscores Chater’s point: if a mindless algorithm can generate convincing rationales, perhaps our own rationales are also post hoc constructions.

## William Forsythe’s Choreographic Thinking: Algorithms of Movement

Moving from language to movement, we encounter another powerful analogy in dance. Choreographer **William Forsythe** treats choreography as a form of thought—*choreographic thinking*—using improvisational structures to generate novel movement.

Forsythe’s practice employs *modalities*—basic movement concepts such as shearing, collapsing, folding, or matching—as building blocks for improvisation. Dancers practice within each modality until it becomes a reminder tool that can trigger variation. By recombining and switching between modalities, dancers explore a vast space of possible movements.

Each modality functions as a **vector**: a directional constraint that shapes movement without prescribing a fixed outcome. Within and between these vectors, dancers improvise freely, producing unique sequences.

Forsythe has described some of his instructions as *“little language machines”*—verbal algorithms that generate movement. A proposition like *“drop a curve”* forces dancers to reconfigure habitual movement patterns, producing contrast and novelty. These instructions are enabling constraints, not rigid formulas; they *“do not insist on a single path to form-of-thought.”*

The concept of the **choreographic object** extends this logic further. Forsythe asks whether choreography can generate autonomous expressions of its principles without the body—akin to a musical score. Choreography, in this sense, models potential transitions from one state to another in any imaginable space. This mirrors how an LLM encodes potential transitions between linguistic states in latent space.

## Vectorial Improvisation: Bridging Language and Dance

Across neural networks, poetry, and choreography, a unifying principle emerges: **vectorial improvisation**.

- There is a high-dimensional space of possibilities  
- A path is carved through it by sequential choices  
- Each choice is constrained by context yet reshapes future possibilities  

The process is linear in execution but vectorial in design. Improvisation means the path is not predetermined; it is discovered through traversal.

In LLMs, different random seeds yield different continuations. In improvisational music or dance, performers choose among many possible next actions. Forsythe’s dancer executing *“drop a curve”* may realize it differently each time. Both AI text generation and human improvisation involve real-time navigation of possibility space.

In all cases, internalized patterns enable coherence, while deviation enables novelty. Williams’s call for a *new mind* speaks directly to this tension. Introducing randomness or constraints in AI, or modalities in dance, disrupts habitual paths and forces invention.

There is also a reflexive loop at work. A new poetic line reshapes how earlier lines are read. A surprising AI response reframes a dialogue. An unexpected dance movement reorients the performance. Each step both emerges from and produces a new state—a new *mind*. The line changes the mind as much as the mind produces the line.

---

## Conclusion

Intelligence and creativity—whether human or artificial—can be understood as improvisational traversal through structured spaces of possibility. Large language models literalize this process through vector embeddings and sequential prediction. Human poets enact it through shifts in perception. William Forsythe embodies it through choreographic systems that turn movement into thinking.

In all cases, linear sequences mask deeper vectorial logics. Each step is a point in a high-dimensional landscape, and creativity lies in choosing an unexpected path. The *“mind is flat”* perspective reinforces this view: reasoning may be less about accessing depth and more about navigating surfaces.

Ultimately, the poet, the algorithm, and the choreographer converge on a shared lesson: the frontier of the possible is discovered one step at a time. Truly new lines of thought—poetic, computational, or corporeal—require the courage to break from the old and venture into uncharted space.
