## Large Language Models, Poetics, and Choreographic Thinking

## Introduction

Modern large language models (LLMs) generate text by traversing a high-dimensional **latent space** of language in a sequential, stepwise fashion. Each new word or line produced by an LLM can be understood as a step along a vector in this latent space. This process bears a striking conceptual resemblance to human creativity and improvisation.

Poet **William Carlos Williams** famously asserted that *“unless there is a new mind there cannot be a new line”*, highlighting how a shift in mindset is required to produce truly original language. Similarly, choreographer **William Forsythe’s** experimental dance practices treat the body as a *thinking tool* capable of generating novelty through algorithmic improvisation. In both poetic creation and choreographic experimentation—and, as this essay argues, in LLM-driven text generation—we encounter a shared logic: a linear sequence of actions or expressions emerging from a multidimensional space of possibilities, guided by constraints, context, and invention.

This essay explores the conceptual continuities between:
- Williams’s poetics of the **new line / new mind**
- the **vectorial, predictive structure** of LLMs
- contemporary theories of **finite and improvisational cognition**
- and Forsythe’s **choreographic thinking**, where movement functions as a form of spatial reasoning.

Together, these perspectives allow us to frame cognition—human and synthetic—not as depth excavation, but as **procedural traversal** through structured spaces of potential.

---

## Large Language Models: Sequential Trajectories in Latent Space

Large language models operate not on words as such, but on **vector embeddings**: numerical representations of linguistic units situated in a high-dimensional latent space. Tokens that appear in similar contexts occupy nearby regions of this space, while semantic differences correspond to directions and distances.

Text generation in an LLM unfolds as a **sequential traversal** of this space. At each step, the model:
1. Encodes the current context into a vector state  
2. Predicts a probability distribution over possible next tokens  
3. Selects one token (deterministically or stochastically)  
4. Updates the context and repeats  

The model does not plan an entire paragraph in advance. Instead, it **improvises locally**, one token at a time, constrained by prior context and learned statistical regularities. Meaning, in this framework, is not stored as a symbolic structure but **emerges dynamically** as the model moves through latent space.

This process can be understood as **vectorial linearity**:  
- linear in time (token by token)  
- vectorial in structure (each step is a directional move in a multidimensional space)

The result is language as motion—an unfolding path rather than a retrieved object.

## “A New Line Is a New Mind”: Poetics and the Finite Mind

Williams’s claim that a *new line requires a new mind* originates in *Paterson*, where he warns that without cognitive renewal, language will merely repeat itself with “recurring deadliness.” The line is not just a formal unit; it is an **event of cognition**.

For Williams, poetic innovation could not be achieved through surface-level formal tricks alone. It required a reorientation of perception—*a new way of seeing*. The finite human mind, shaped by habit and convention, must undergo a shift in attention to produce genuinely new language.

This insight resonates with contemporary understandings of creativity as **re-vectoring** within a constrained cognitive space. Human thought does not generate ideas ex nihilo; it recombines, redirects, and reframes existing materials. Novelty arises not from infinite depth, but from **unexpected trajectories** through familiar terrain.

The same applies to LLMs. Without intervention, a model will default to high-probability continuations—statistical averages of its training data. To generate novel text, one must induce a “new mind” in the system by:
- increasing temperature  
- altering prompts  
- introducing unusual constraints  

These techniques shift the model’s path in latent space, allowing new lines to emerge.

## The Improvising Brain and the “Flat Mind”

Cognitive scientist **Nick Chater**, in *The Mind Is Flat*, challenges the intuition that the mind contains deep, stable structures of belief and intention. Instead, he argues that cognition is largely **improvisational**: we construct thoughts, decisions, and explanations on the fly, using available cues and narratives.

According to this view:
- there is no vast inner storehouse of hidden beliefs  
- reasoning is a form of real-time storytelling  
- metacognition itself may be a secondary improvisation  

This perspective aligns uncannily with how LLMs operate. A language model has no underlying self or coherent worldview. When prompted to explain its reasoning, it generates a plausible explanation because it has learned how explanations *sound*, not because it accessed a hidden chain of thought.

Human cognition and LLM cognition thus converge in an unexpected way: both may rely more on **surface-level sequential construction** than on deep internal representations. In both cases, coherence emerges through **context-sensitive continuation**, not from consulting a pre-existing inner model.

## William Forsythe and Choreographic Thinking

William Forsythe extends these ideas from language into movement. His concept of **choreographic thinking** treats choreography not as fixed form but as a system for generating possibilities.

In works such as *Improvisation Technologies* and *Choreographic Objects*, Forsythe introduces:
- **modalities** (e.g. folding, shearing, aligning)
- verbal propositions that function as **movement algorithms**
- scores that define *spaces of potential transition* rather than prescribed sequences

Dancers operating within these systems do not execute predefined steps. Instead, they **navigate a space of constraints**, making real-time decisions that generate novel movement paths. Each action redefines the conditions for the next.

Forsythe’s choreography thus functions as:
- an embodied algorithm  
- a procedural exploration of space  
- a form of non-verbal reasoning  

Movement becomes thought in action.

## Vectorial Improvisation Across Domains

Across poetry, AI, and choreography, we find a shared structure:

- a **space of possibilities** (semantic, cognitive, or physical)
- a set of **constraints or rules**
- a **sequential traversal** through that space
- and an emergent form produced by improvisation

In all three domains:
- execution is linear (one word, one thought, one movement at a time)
- structure is vectorial (each step is a directional choice)
- novelty arises from deviation, not randomness alone  

A poetic line, a generated token, or a danced gesture each functions as:
- a response to the current state
- and a transformation of that state  

The system—human or artificial—continuously incorporates its own output as new input. This reflexivity is central to both creativity and metacognition.

## Conclusion

Seen through this interdisciplinary lens, intelligence and creativity—whether human or synthetic—appear less as expressions of depth and more as **procedural improvisations**. The mind, like an LLM or a dancer, operates by navigating structured spaces through sequential decisions.

William Carlos Williams’s dictum *“a new line is a new mind”* captures this dynamic with poetic precision. A new line does not merely express a thought; it **creates a new cognitive state** from which further possibilities unfold.

Large language models literalize this process through vectorial computation. William Forsythe embodies it through choreographic systems. Cognitive science reframes it through theories of improvisation and flat cognition.

Together, they suggest a powerful rethinking of mind, language, and creativity:  
not as excavation of hidden depths, but as **movement through possibility**.

The frontier of thought is not buried deep within—it is discovered step by step,  
**in the act of going forward**.
